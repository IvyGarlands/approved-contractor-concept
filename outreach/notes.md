# Things I would not have thought of

Working notes to have open during the call or while you're deciding whether
to send this at all. Nothing here is customer-facing.

---

## The honesty pass

Everything on the concept site that is placeholder, recreated, or
unverified — so you're never caught flat-footed in a conversation.

**Recreated, not theirs:**
- **The wordmark.** The stamp mark on the concept site is a redrawn version
  of their real logo idea, built from colors sampled directly off their
  actual logo file (`#ae0f15` red, `#231f20` ink). It is not their actual
  logo asset — we never obtained their high-resolution original. If they
  ask "is that our logo," the honest answer is: "It's your colors and your
  idea, redrawn properly. If you have the original file, I can match it
  exactly or use it as-is."

**Real, not placeholder — worth knowing so you don't undersell it:**
- All 11 photographs on the concept site are **their own real completed
  jobs**, pulled from their own site's featured-projects pages (Rancho
  Palos Verdes, La Jolla, Encino, and others). None of it is stock.
- The license record card (status, issue date, bond, workers' comp) is
  **pulled directly from the CSLB state register**, not from their
  marketing. It's real and it's current as of the lookup date shown on the
  card.
- The reviews shown are **verbatim**, sourced from GuildQuality (which
  surveys customers directly) and their own site.

**Deliberately left off — say this is a choice, not an oversight, if asked:**
- **No star rating or review count anywhere.** Their own site claims
  "800+ reviews." GuildQuality shows 4.6 from 85. Houzz shows 4.7 from 77.
  Yelp shows 125. A fourth source claims 258 and is provably contaminated
  (it attributes a review of a different company to them). None of these
  numbers agree, so the concept shows individual real reviews instead of a
  number nobody can verify. If Jared asks why there's no star rating, that
  is the honest answer, and it's a good one to give in person.
- **No hours.** Not published anywhere reliable. Third-party listings
  disagree with each other on Saturday hours specifically.
- **No portrait of Jared.** None exists publicly. This is the single
  highest-value asset missing from the whole build — see the photo list
  below.

**Facts I still need him to confirm, before this goes any further:**
- **Financing terms.** "12 months no interest" was read off their own site
  on 2026-08-12. Promotional finance terms move constantly. Confirm the
  current offer before it's republished anywhere.
- **James Hardie Elite Contractor.** This is their own published claim. I
  could not independently verify it (their manufacturer-directory page is
  behind a bot-check I didn't bypass). If he can't produce current proof of
  it, it should come off.
- **Qualified Remodeler Top 500 / HIP 200 badges.** Both say "2025." Confirm
  they're current for 2026 before this ships anywhere permanent.
- **BBB rating.** It's a real A+ rating, but the same BBB profile also
  states they are **not** a BBB Accredited Business. Those are two
  different things. Never say "BBB Accredited," only "A+ rating."

## Objection prep

The five things Jared is most likely to say, and a plain, honest answer to
each.

**1. "We already have a website. Someone handles that for us."**
This is the most likely objection, not a hypothetical one. Their current
site runs on a real, actively maintained plugin stack (WP Rocket, a
security plugin, an SEO plugin, a redirect manager, and hosting-specific
optimization plugins that only make sense on SiteGround) and was modified
two days before this outreach was written. Somebody is being paid to touch
this site regularly. **Honest answer:** "That's completely fine, I'm not
asking you to fire anyone. This is just a second opinion you didn't ask
for. If you like it better, what you do with your current setup is
entirely your call, not mine."

**2. "What's the catch? Why so cheap?"**
**Honest answer:** exactly what's on the deck. The site is already built,
so the price reflects finishing an existing thing, not starting one from
nothing. There is no catch, no upsell buried in fine print, and no
retainer required to get the $700 version.

**3. "What if we're just not interested?"**
**Honest answer, and also the instruction to yourself:** nothing happens.
No follow-up beyond the one message in the follow-up section below, no
guilt, no "just checking in" every month. Say plainly that ignoring this is
genuinely fine, because it is.

**4. "We want this, but can't pay $700 all at once right now."**
**Honest answer:** offer to split it, two payments, first half to start the
content swap, second half at handoff. Or start with the $99/mo plan and
fold the one-time setup into the first month or two rather than as a lump
sum. Either is a completely reasonable ask and worth agreeing to on the
spot rather than treating as a negotiation.

**5. "How do I know this is legitimate, not a scam?"**
Fair question for a cold email with a link in it, especially in this trade,
where "we treat your home like it's our own" is a direct response to being
burned before. **Honest answer:** no payment is requested before any work
starts or is discussed, the concept is already live and inspectable with
nothing hidden behind a login, and you're happy to get on a call first.
Offering a call before any money changes hands is itself the proof.

## Deliverability

**Send the first email as plain text, not HTML, from a personal Gmail
address, one at a time, by hand.** Not through a cold-email or mail-merge
tool. A personal-to-personal email between two Gmail-style addresses has a
much better delivery rate than anything that looks automated, and this
whole approach is built around not looking automated.

**Links: one, total.** Just the live concept URL. Don't also drop the deck
link into the first email, that's two links competing for attention and
doubles the spam-filter surface for no reason.

**No tracking pixels.** None were built into the deck (fonts and the one
screenshot are embedded directly in the file, there are no external image
or script calls of any kind). Keep it that way. Don't run the email through
an open-tracking tool either, for the same reason plus the fact that it
would be a strange thing to do to someone you're explicitly telling "you
don't owe me anything."

**No link shorteners.** Use the real domain, spelled out. A shortened link
on a cold email from an unfamiliar sender is one of the more reliable ways
to get flagged, and it also just looks a little off for "a person in
Thousand Oaks who noticed something."

**Don't attach the PDF deck on the first send.** A cold email with an
attachment from a first-time sender is a stronger spam signal than a link,
and most mail providers are more suspicious of unsolicited attachments than
unsolicited links. **Recommendation: hold the deck back entirely for the
first message.** If Jared replies and shows interest, send the deck as an
attachment in that reply thread, where it's expected and welcome rather
than cold. If you want pricing in front of him sooner than a reply, a
follow-up email a few days after the first one (see below) is a better
place for it than the very first message.

---

## The follow-up

Send once, about a week after the first message, only if there's been no
reply. Written to be genuinely easy to ignore a second time.

> Hi Jared, just following up in case this got buried, totally fine if
> not. The site's still up if you want to take a look:
> [LINK]. Won't follow up again after this either way.

**Stop after this one.** No third message, no "final follow-up," no
different channel after this. If there's no reply to the follow-up, the
honest read is that it's a no, and that's a complete and acceptable answer.

## What to photograph, if he says yes

By section, in priority order:

1. **A portrait of Jared.** The single highest-value photo missing from
   everything gathered about this business. A natural shot works better
   than a stiff headshot for this trade, on a job site, at a consultation,
   whatever he's got. This alone would meaningfully improve the site.
2. **One flagship hero photo.** Their best, most recent completed exterior
   job, wide shot, good light, no people in frame, landscape orientation.
   Ideally something from the last few months, not a project already shown
   on their current site.
3. **One clear photo per service** (siding, windows, paint/coating,
   roofing): a finished shot that unambiguously shows that specific trade,
   for the service tiles.
4. **4 to 8 more finished exteriors** for the work/gallery section, a mix
   of home styles across the areas they actually serve.
5. **At least two real before/after pairs**, same angle of the same house,
   shot before work started and again after it finished. This is the
   single most persuasive thing an exterior contractor can show, and it's
   the one type of photo that can't be recreated after the fact, so it's
   worth asking for existing ones from recent jobs rather than waiting for
   a new project to document.
6. **Optional, lower priority:** a crew or team photo, a branded vehicle,
   physical award or certification documents if they photograph well.

## Whether the concept should be shown at all

**Nothing found here is a reason not to send this.** The license is real,
current, and clean, no disclosable complaints on the state record. The
owner is an active, reachable, real person who engages with his own
customers and his own Facebook page. The reviews, including the ones
deliberately not published anywhere, describe a business with normal,
minor service issues (some scheduling delays, one property-damage
complaint), not a pattern of anything that should give you pause about
approaching them.

**Two things worth knowing, not reasons to stop:**
- The existing site is actively vendor-managed, not neglected. This raises
  the odds of the "we already have someone" objection, handled above, but
  it doesn't make outreach inappropriate, it just means going in with that
  answer ready.
- The reviews are good but not spotless. Don't oversell "everyone loves
  them" in conversation. The honest, calibrated version, which is also
  what the concept site itself already does, is stronger than a polished
  one would be anyway.

If anything changes this read, it would be new information from the actual
conversation, not anything visible from here.
