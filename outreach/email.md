Fill in `[LINK]` and `[YOUR PHONE]` before sending anything. The link is the
live concept site; do not use a `pages.dev` or `vercel.app` staging link if
you have a real domain up by send time. See the deliverability notes in
`notes.md` before you send the first one.

## Why it's built this way

**The opening line is Kimberly A.'s "cotton candy pink puff" quote**,
pulled verbatim from `reviews.txt`. It's the single most specific,
unmistakable thing available: nobody could paste that sentence into an
email to a different business, and if Jared reads it he'll know exactly
which customer and which job it's from. It also does double duty, proving
both that the reviews were actually read and that people write real,
specific things about this business.

**The second beat, "people keep thanking you by name,"** is backed by three
separate reviewers naming Jared directly and unprompted (Kimberly A.,
Jeffrey K., Tracy G. — see `contact.md`). That pattern is unusual enough to
be worth naming: it's true of a small, owner-run shop in a way it wouldn't
be true of a company with a call center.

**The one flaw named is the mobile chat-popup stack**, verified live today
by loading the actual site at a 375px viewport: a "Live Person here to
help" popup launches automatically and sits directly over the hero button,
stacked on top of a permanent sticky bar and a floating chat-avatar bubble.
This is not a design opinion, it's a screenshot-able fact, and it's the
single flaw most likely to be costing real money, since most of this
business's incoming traffic is someone on a phone deciding whether to tap
"call."

**What was deliberately left out of the flaw line:** the (866)/(877) phone
number swap. It's very likely just CallRail doing dynamic number insertion
correctly, not a mistake, so naming it as a flaw would have been
speculative and slightly show-off-y about research depth that doesn't
actually point to a problem. Save it for a live conversation if it comes
up, don't put an unproven claim in a first message.
